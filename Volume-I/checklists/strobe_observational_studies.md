# D.2 STROBE: Observational Studies

From Appendix D of *Modern Biostatistics for the Health Sciences, Volume I*.

Use when reporting a cohort, case-control, or cross-sectional study; the authoritative checklist and its design-specific variants are published by the STROBE initiative at strobe-statement.org and should be downloaded from there before submission, the domains below are summarized in our own words.

The title and abstract name the study design with a commonly used term, such as cohort or case-control.

The Introduction gives the scientific background and states specific objectives, including any pre-specified hypotheses.

Key elements of the design are presented early in the Methods rather than left for the reader to infer.

The setting, locations, and relevant dates are given, covering recruitment, exposure, follow-up, and data collection.

Eligibility criteria and the sources and methods of participant selection are described for every group in the study.

Outcomes, exposures, predictors, and potential confounders are clearly defined, with diagnostic criteria where relevant.

For each variable, the data source and the method of measurement are given, and comparability across groups is addressed.

Efforts to address potential sources of bias are described in the Methods, not merely conceded in the Discussion.

How the study size was arrived at is explained, whether by calculation, by availability, or by the size of the source data.

How quantitative variables were handled, including any grouping of continuous measures, is stated with the reason.

All statistical methods are described, including control of confounding, subgroup analyses, and handling of missing data.

Participant numbers are reported at each stage, with reasons for non-participation, ideally in a flow diagram.

Unadjusted and confounder-adjusted estimates are both reported, with their precision and the confounders included.

The Discussion states limitations, likely direction of bias, generalizability, funding, and the role of the funders.
