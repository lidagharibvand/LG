# D.1 CONSORT 2025: Randomized Trials

From Appendix D of *Modern Biostatistics for the Health Sciences, Volume I*.

Use when reporting a randomized trial. CONSORT 2025 supersedes CONSORT 2010 and contains the authoritative 30-item checklist and participant-flow diagram. Download the current checklist from consort-spirit.org and use it with any relevant extension. What follows is an orientation to its main domains, not official checklist wording.

The title or abstract identifies the study as randomized, so that it can be found as a trial.

The Introduction gives the scientific background and states the specific objectives or pre-specified hypotheses.

The Methods describe the trial design, including the allocation ratio and any change made after the trial began.

Eligibility criteria for participants, and the settings and locations where data were collected, are described in full.

Interventions in each arm are described in enough detail to permit replication, including how and when they were given.

Primary and secondary outcomes are completely defined, with how and when each one was assessed.

The sample size is justified, and any interim analyses and stopping rules are explained.

The method of generating the random allocation sequence is described, along with any restriction such as blocking.

Allocation concealment is described: who generated the sequence, who enrolled participants, and who assigned them to arms.

Blinding is reported, who was blinded after assignment, and how the similarity of the interventions was maintained.

A flow diagram gives the numbers screened, randomized, receiving the intervention, lost to follow-up, and analyzed.

Each outcome is reported with an effect size, a measure of precision such as a confidence interval, and its denominator.

All important harms and unintended effects in each arm are reported, not only the favorable outcomes.

Limitations, generalizability, the trial registration number, access to the full protocol, and funding sources are stated.
