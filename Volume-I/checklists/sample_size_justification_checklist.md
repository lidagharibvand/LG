# D.8 Sample-Size Justification Checklist

From Appendix D of *Modern Biostatistics for the Health Sciences, Volume I*.

An original checklist drafted for this volume from the five elements in Section 12.7: use it to audit your own justification paragraph, or a colleague’s, before a protocol, grant application, or manuscript is submitted.

The primary outcome is named, and it is the outcome the sample size was computed for, not a secondary one.

The statistical test or model that will analyze the primary outcome is named, and the formula used matches it.

The assumed effect size is stated in the units of the outcome, not only as a standardized effect size.

The source of the effect size is cited: prior literature, pilot data, or a minimum clinically important difference.

The assumed variability, standard deviation, or baseline event rate is stated, with its own source cited.

The significance level is given, and the test is identified as one-tailed or two-tailed with a stated reason.

The target power is stated, and it is the power to detect the effect size named above rather than some other effect.

The allocation ratio is stated, and any efficiency loss from unequal allocation is accounted for in the number.

Inflation for dropout or non-response is applied, with the expected rate justified from comparable prior studies.

Clustering, where present, is handled with a stated intra-cluster correlation and an average cluster size.

Any multiplicity adjustment is applied to the significance level used in the calculation, not bolted on afterward.

Both the analyzable and the enrolled numbers are reported, and the arithmetic between them can be reproduced.

The software and version used are named, and the calculation has been reproduced in a second independent run.

A sensitivity table or statement shows how the required number changes under alternative plausible assumptions.
