# D.3 PRISMA 2020: Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses

From Appendix D of *Modern Biostatistics for the Health Sciences, Volume I*.

Use when reporting a systematic review or meta-analysis; the authoritative checklist, abstract checklist, and flow diagram are maintained by the PRISMA group at prisma-statement.org and should be downloaded before the review begins rather than after it ends, this is our paraphrase of its domains.

The title identifies the report as a systematic review, and as a meta-analysis where results were pooled.

The review question is stated in terms of the populations, interventions, comparators, and outcomes it covers.

The protocol was registered, for example in PROSPERO, and the registration number and any amendments are reported.

Eligibility criteria for including and excluding studies are specified, including how reports were grouped for synthesis.

All information sources are named, with the date on which each one was last searched.

The full search strategy for at least one database is presented, including every filter and limit that was applied.

The selection process is described: how many reviewers screened, whether independently, and how disagreements were resolved.

The data-collection process and the list of data items sought from each included study are described.

The method used to assess risk of bias in each included study is named, along with who applied it and how.

Synthesis methods are described, including the effect measure, the model used, and how heterogeneity was assessed.

Methods for assessing reporting bias and for rating certainty of evidence, such as GRADE, are specified.

A flow diagram reports the numbers of records screened, assessed for eligibility, excluded with reasons, and included.

Characteristics and risk-of-bias assessments for every included study are presented, not only pooled results.

The Discussion covers limitations of the evidence and of the review process, funding, competing interests, and data availability.
