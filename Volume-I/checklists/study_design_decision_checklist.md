# D.7 Study-Design Decision Checklist

From Appendix D of *Modern Biostatistics for the Health Sciences, Volume I*.

An original checklist drafted for this volume: work through it before committing to a design, and keep your written answers, because they become the first draft of your Methods section and of your protocol.

The research question is written in PICO form, with population, exposure or intervention, comparator, and outcome each named.

The claim I want to make at the end is written out as a sentence, and I have checked which design would license it.

I have decided in writing whether this question is descriptive, associational, or causal, and the design matches that choice.

I have established whether randomization is ethical and feasible here, and recorded the reason if it is not.

The target population and the sampling frame are distinct in my plan, and the gap between them is named openly.

The unit of assignment and the unit of analysis are both stated, and any mismatch between them is accounted for.

The primary outcome is single, pre-specified, and defined by instrument, timing, assessor, and handling of missing values.

The comparator is a real alternative that participants would otherwise receive, not a straw comparison chosen to win.

The principal threats to internal validity are listed by name, each paired with the design feature that addresses it.

The populations to whom this result will not generalize are named now, before data collection rather than after it.

The analysis that will answer the primary question is specified, and it suits the level of measurement of the outcome.

The sample size required by that analysis has been computed, and I have confirmed it is achievable in my setting.

The reporting guideline this study will follow is identified, and I have read it before collecting any data.

The analysis plan is pre-registered, or the protocol is filed and dated, before the data are examined.
